Deepfake generation techniques leave behind subtle visual inconsistencies — artefacts that aren't obvious to the human eye but are learnable by a model trained specifically to look for them. This project set out to build that model: a spatio-temporal classifier that combines a Vision Transformer (ViT) for per-frame feature extraction with a BiLSTM temporal head to capture how those artefacts evolve across frames.

Trained and evaluated on the FaceForensics++ (FaceSwap) benchmark, the model reached 94.67% accuracy, outperforming baseline architectures by up to 8 points. Getting there meant building a proper video preprocessing pipeline first: RetinaFace-based face detection and cropping paired with multithreaded frame sampling to turn raw FaceForensics++ video into model-ready training data at a reasonable throughput.

Working as a three-person team meant splitting the pipeline into clear stages: preprocessing and dataset preparation, model architecture (ViT + BiLSTM) and training, and evaluation. Coordinating those handoffs cleanly was almost as much a part of the project as the modeling itself.
